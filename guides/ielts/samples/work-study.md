## Part 1: Introduction & Interview

**Q: Do you work or are you a student?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Currently, I’m a senior at ABC University, majoring in Information Technology. I’m in my final year, so I’m quite busy preparing for my graduation thesis.

**Q: Why did you choose that job/major?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Well, I’ve always been tech-savvy since I was a kid. I chose IT because I believe technology is the future, and this field offers promising career prospects with a lucrative income.

**Q: How do you balance work/studies with your personal life?**
*   **Band 8.0:** That’s a tough one, but I try to set strict boundaries. When I’m at work, I’m 100% focused, but once I clock out, I disconnect completely. I dedicate my evenings and weekends to my hobbies and loved ones to recharge my batteries.

## Part 2: Cue Card (Long Turn)

**Describe a job you would like to have in the future.**

You should say:
*   What it is
*   How you heard about it
*   What qualifications are needed
*   And explain why you would like to have this job.

### 💡 Idea & Structure (PPF Method)

*   **Past (Introduction):** Ever since I watched the movie "The Social Network" back in high school, I’ve been obsessed with the idea of becoming a Software Architect.
*   **Present (Description):** It’s a senior role that involves designing complex software systems and making high-level design choices. To get there, I need to have a solid foundation in computer science, master several programming languages, and probably get a few years of hands-on coding experience under my belt.
*   **Future (Conclusion):** I aspire to this role because it combines creativity with logic. I want to build systems that can scale and impact millions of users. Plus, let's be honest, the remuneration package for this position is extremely attractive, which would give me financial freedom.

### 🗣️ Sample Answer (Band 8.0+)
"I’ve always harbored a dream of becoming a **Software Architect** for a major tech giant like Google or Amazon. Essentially, this role is like being the architect of a building, but for software. You decide the structure, the technologies to be used, and how different parts of the system interact.

I first became interested in this path when I started coding in university and realized that writing code is just one part of the puzzle; designing how that code fits together is the real challenge.

To **land this job**, you need a **robust technical background**, usually a degree in Computer Science, and extensive experience in software development. Soft skills like leadership and communication are also paramount because you have to explain complex technical concepts to non-technical stakeholders.

I’m drawn to this career for two main reasons. Firstly, it offers a **stimulating intellectual challenge**. I enjoy solving complex problems and optimizing systems for efficiency. Secondly, it’s a very **future-proof career** with **lucrative salary prospects**. Being a Software Architect would allow me to leave a mark on the digital world while securing a comfortable life for myself and my family."

## Part 3: Discussion

**Q: Which jobs are most respected in your country?**
*   **Band 8.0:** In Vietnam, traditional professions like **doctors and teachers** are still held in high esteem. Doctors are respected because they save lives, while teachers are valued for molding the future generation. However, recently, tech entrepreneurs and IT professionals are also gaining significant respect due to their contributions to the economy and innovation.

**Q: Do you think money is the most important factor when choosing a job?**
*   **Band 8.0:** While a **competitive salary** is undeniably important to cover living expenses, I don't think it should be the sole deciding factor. **Job satisfaction** and a positive work environment are equally crucial. If you earn a fortune but dread going to work every day, you’ll eventually burn out. So, I believe a balance between income and passion is the key.

**Q: How has the job market changed in recent years?**
*   **Band 8.0:** The landscape has shifted dramatically with the rise of **remote work** and the **gig economy**. Thanks to technology, people are no longer tethered to a physical office and can work from anywhere. We also see a surge in demand for digital skills like data analysis and AI, while some manual jobs are becoming obsolete due to automation.
