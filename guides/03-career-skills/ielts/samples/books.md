# 📚 Topic: Books & Reading

## Part 1: Introduction & Interview

**Q: Do you enjoy reading books?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Absolutely. Reading is my way to unwind and also to broaden my horizons. I usually read nonfiction on weekdays and fiction on weekends.

**Q: What kind of books do you like?**
*   **Band 8.0:** I gravitate towards biographies and behavioral science. They give me insight into how people make decisions and overcome setbacks.

**Q: Do you prefer e-books or printed books?**
*   **Band 8.0:** For convenience, e-books are great—I can highlight and sync across devices. But for deep work, I still love the feel of a printed book because it’s less distracting.

## Part 2: Cue Card (Long Turn)

**Describe a book that changed your perspective.**

You should say:
*   What the book is about
*   When you read it
*   What ideas impressed you
*   And explain how it changed your thinking

### 💡 Idea & Structure (PPF Method)
*   **Past:** Picked up "Atomic Habits" during a stressful semester.
*   **Present:** Key idea: tiny improvements compound; system > goals; environment design.
*   **Future:** Applied habit stacking; plan to revisit yearly to reinforce routines.

### 🗣️ Sample Answer (Band 8.0+)
"A book that reshaped my mindset is **Atomic Habits** by James Clear. I read it when I was juggling exams and part-time work. The author argues that small, **incremental changes** outperform grand resolutions. What struck me most was the idea of building **systems instead of goals** and designing your environment to make good habits effortless.

After finishing the book, I started **habit stacking**—linking a new habit to an existing one. For instance, after brewing my morning coffee, I do ten minutes of vocabulary drills. The results were subtle at first but **compounded** over months. I plan to reread it every year as a reminder that consistent, tiny actions can lead to **outsized** results."

## Part 3: Discussion

**Q: Are paper books still important in the digital age?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Yes. Printed books encourage deep focus because there are no push notifications. Libraries also serve as community hubs that digital platforms can’t replace.

**Q: Should children be encouraged to read fiction or nonfiction?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Both. Fiction builds empathy and imagination, while nonfiction develops critical thinking. A balanced diet keeps them curious and informed.

**Q: How can governments promote reading?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Invest in public libraries, run reading challenges in schools, and subsidize local authors. Making books affordable and visible is key.
