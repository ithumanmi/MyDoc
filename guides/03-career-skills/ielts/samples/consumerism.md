## Part 1: Introduction & Interview

**Q: How often do you go shopping?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Maybe once a month for essentials. I try to avoid impulse buying.

**Q: Do you prefer shopping online or in-store?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Online for convenience and price comparisons, but in-store for clothes because I want to check the fit and material.

**Q: What do you spend most of your money on?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Mostly on books, groceries, and occasionally gadgets. I track expenses to stay within budget.

---

## Part 2: Cue Card (Long Turn)

**Describe a purchase you regret.**

You should say:
*   What you bought
*   Why you bought it
*   Why you regret it
*   And explain what you learned from it

### 💡 Idea & Structure (PPF Method)
*   **Past:** Bought a trendy gadget on sale.
*   **Present:** Rarely used; took space; felt wasteful.
*   **Future:** Now apply a 30-day rule before buying.

### 🗣️ Sample Answer (Band 8.0+)
"I regret buying a **smartwatch** that was on a flash sale. I was attracted by the discount and the sleek design. After the excitement faded, I realized I barely used its features. It sat in a drawer, which made me feel wasteful.

Since then, I use a **30-day rule**: if I still want the item after a month, I’ll consider it. That habit has saved me from impulse buys and kept my spending **intentional**."

---

## Part 3: Discussion

**Q: Why do people buy things they don’t need?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Advertising triggers emotions and social pressure. Discounts also create a fear of missing out.

**Q: Should governments regulate advertising more strictly?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Yes, especially for products targeting children or promoting unhealthy habits. Transparency about data usage is also necessary.

**Q: How can consumers shop more sustainably?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Buy less but better quality, choose durable items, repair instead of replace, and support brands with ethical supply chains.

- **budget tracker**
- **ethical supply chain**
- **planned obsolescence**
