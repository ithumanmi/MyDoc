## Part 1: Introduction & Interview

**Q: Do you live in a house or an apartment?**
*   **Band 8.0:** I live in a small apartment near the city center. It’s compact but convenient—close to the metro and supermarkets.

**Q: What do you like about your neighborhood?**
*   **Band 8.0:** It’s walkable and has plenty of cafés. The only downside is the occasional noise at night.

**Q: Would you prefer to live in the city or the countryside?**
*   **Band 8.0:** For now, the city suits me because of work opportunities and amenities. Maybe later I’ll move somewhere quieter.

---

## Part 2: Cue Card (Long Turn)

**Describe a place you have lived that you really liked.**

You should say:
*   Where it was
*   What the accommodation was like
*   Why you liked living there
*   And explain whether you would live there again

### 💡 Idea & Structure (PPF Method)
*   **Past:** Lived in a serviced apartment during an internship.
*   **Present:** Great location, shared gym, quiet; small kitchen but manageable.
*   **Future:** Would live again for convenience; maybe seek better soundproofing.

### 🗣️ Sample Answer (Band 8.0+)
"During an internship, I lived in a **serviced apartment** near the downtown business district. The unit was compact but well-designed, with lots of built-in storage. I loved the **walkability**—grocery stores, a metro station, and a public park were all within five minutes.

The building had a shared **gym** and good security, which made me feel safe. The only drawback was the thin walls, so I bought **noise-cancelling** headphones. Overall, I’d live there again because the convenience outweighed the minor downsides. It taught me that smart layout matters more than sheer size."

---

## Part 3: Discussion

**Q: What challenges do people face when living in big cities?**
*   **Band 8.0:** High rent, noise pollution, and long commutes. However, cities offer better access to jobs and services.

**Q: Should governments invest more in public housing?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Yes, to keep housing affordable and reduce homelessness. Mixed-income housing can also improve social integration.

**Q: How can urban design improve quality of life?**
*   **Band 8.0:** More green spaces, pedestrian zones, and reliable transit reduce stress and pollution. Mixed-use buildings also create vibrant communities.

## 📚 Essential Vocabulary for Housing & Urban Living

- **serviced apartment / studio flat**
- **walkable / amenities**
- **noise-cancelling / soundproofing**
- **mixed-use development**
- **public housing / affordable housing**
- **rent spike / mortgage**
- **urban sprawl / high-density**
- **green space / pedestrian zone**
- **commute / transit-oriented**

---

## 🎯 Why Band 8.0+
- Clear contrast: convenience vs. downsides (noise, size)
- Concrete urban design terms (mixed-use, transit-oriented)
- Practical takeaways (layout > size, soundproofing)
