## Part 1: Introduction & Interview

**Q: What is your earliest childhood memory?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Playing in my grandparents’ yard and climbing a mango tree. The smell of ripe fruit is still vivid to me.

**Q: Did your parents have strict rules when you were a child?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Yes, especially about homework and bedtime. Looking back, it taught me discipline.

**Q: What games did you play when you were small?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Mostly hide-and-seek and simple outdoor games with neighbors. They didn’t require gadgets—just imagination.

---

## Part 2: Cue Card (Long Turn)

**Describe a happy memory from your childhood.**

You should say:
*   When and where it happened
*   Who was involved
*   What you did
*   And explain why it was memorable

### 💡 Idea & Structure (PPF Method)
*   **Past:** Family picnic by a river; simple food; no phones.
*   **Present:** Felt connected; laughter; learned to swim there.
*   **Future:** Want to recreate that for my kids—tech-free weekends.

### 🗣️ Sample Answer (Band 8.0+)
"One of my happiest childhood memories is a **family picnic** by a river when I was about eight. We brought homemade food, played card games, and there were no phones to distract anyone.

I remember learning to **swim** that day with my father’s help. The water was cold, but the laughter kept me warm. It was memorable because everyone was fully present. That experience shapes how I view family time today—I hope to recreate similar **tech-free weekends** with my own children."

---

## Part 3: Discussion

**Q: How have childhoods changed compared to the past?**
*   **Band 8.0:** More screen time and structured activities now; less outdoor play. However, kids today have better access to information.

**Q: Should parents be strict or lenient?**
*   **Band 8.0:** A balance is ideal—clear boundaries with warmth. Strictness without explanation can backfire, while total freedom can cause chaos.

**Q: What values should parents teach their children?**
*   **Band 8.0:** Kindness, responsibility, and curiosity. These help children adapt and contribute to society.
