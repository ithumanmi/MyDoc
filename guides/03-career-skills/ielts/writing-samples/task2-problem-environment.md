# 📝 Problem-Solution Essay: Climate Change

> [← Back to Writing Samples Index](./README.md)

## 📌 Question:

**"Climate change is now a major threat to life on Earth. What do you think are the main causes of this problem? What measures can be taken to address it?"**

**Essay Type:** Problem-Solution (Two-Part Question)
**Recommended Time:** 40 minutes
**Word Count Target:** 270-290 words

---

## ❌ Band 6.0 Sample (Common Problems Highlighted)

Climate change is a big problem today. **[Weak opening - states the obvious]** There are many reasons for this. **[Too vague - no specific causes]** I will talk about the causes and solutions. **[Mechanical - no sophistication]**

The first cause is pollution from factories. **[Weak topic sentence - too simple]** Factories make a lot of smoke and this goes into the air. **[Simplistic explanation]** This makes the earth hot. **[Inaccurate - oversimplified]** Another reason is people use cars too much. **[Basic vocabulary]** Cars make pollution. **[Repetitive - "pollution" used twice]** Many people drive to work every day. **[Vague example - needs development]**

There are some solutions for this problem. **[Weak topic sentence again]** First, government should make rules. **[Too vague - what rules?]** They can tell factories to stop making pollution. **[Unrealistic - factories can't just stop]** Second, people should use buses instead of cars. **[Underdeveloped idea]** If more people use buses, there will be less cars on the road. **[Grammar: "fewer cars"]** This is good for environment. **[Missing article: "the environment"]**

In conclusion, climate change is caused by factories and cars. **[Repetitive of body]** Government and people need to work together to solve it. **[Vague - no specific call to action]** We must act now to save our planet. **[Cliché - overused phrase]**

**(203 words - too short!)**

---

### 🔴 **Problems in Band 6.0 Essay:**

**Task Response (Band 6.0):**
- ❌ Addresses both parts but ideas underdeveloped
- ❌ Causes too simplistic (factories, cars - obvious)
- ❌ Solutions unrealistic and vague
- ❌ Too short (203 words < 250 minimum)

**Coherence & Cohesion (Band 6.0):**
- ❌ Mechanical paragraphing (First, Second...)
- ❌ Limited range of linkers
- ❌ Repetitive language

**Lexical Resource (Band 6.0):**
- ❌ Basic vocabulary (big, a lot of, good)
- ❌ Word repetition (pollution x3, cars x3)
- ❌ Grammatical errors with articles

**Grammatical Range (Band 6.0):**
- ❌ Mostly simple sentences
- ❌ Limited complexity
- ❌ "Less cars" → should be "fewer cars"

## ✅ Band 8.0-9.0 Sample (With Annotations)

**[Introduction - 45 words]**

**Global warming** [Specific term] has **emerged as** [Formal phrase] one of the most **pressing challenges** [Strong collocation] of the 21st century, with **far-reaching consequences** [Academic phrase - wide-ranging effects] for **ecosystems** [Specific academic term] and human **livelihoods** [Formal - means of living]. This essay will explore the **primary drivers** [Formal - main causes] of this **phenomenon** [Formal noun] and propose **viable solutions** [Strong collocation - practical solutions] to **mitigate its impact** [Academic phrase].

---

**[Body Paragraph 1 - Causes - PEER Structure - 105 words]**

**[P - Point]** The **root causes** [Formal phrase] of climate change **stem primarily from** [Academic phrase - originate from] **anthropogenic activities** [Academic term - human-caused], **chief among which** [Formal phrase - most important] is the **combustion of fossil fuels** [Specific technical term]. **[E - Explanation]** **Coal-fired power plants** [Specific example] and **petroleum-based transportation** [Formal phrase] **release vast quantities of** [Formal phrase] **greenhouse gases** [Specific term], particularly **carbon dioxide** [Scientific term], into the atmosphere, **thereby trapping heat** [Cause-effect structure]. **[E - Example]** **According to** [Formal - citing source] the **Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change** [Specific organization - IPCC], global temperatures have risen by approximately 1.1°C since **pre-industrial times** [Specific timeframe], with **fossil fuel consumption** [Formal phrase] accounting for **over 75%** [Specific data] of these emissions. **[R - Result]** **Consequently** [Formal linker], this **relentless burning** [Strong collocation] has **triggered** [Strong verb] **unprecedented** [Academic adjective - never seen before] **glacial melting** [Specific consequence] and **rising sea levels** [Specific consequence].

---

**[Body Paragraph 2 - Solutions - PEER Structure - 115 words]**

**[P - Point]** **Addressing** [Formal verb] this crisis **necessitates** [Formal - requires] a **multi-faceted approach** [Academic phrase - many-sided strategy] **involving both** [Formal structure] **governmental intervention** [Formal phrase] and **individual responsibility** [Formal phrase]. **[E - Explanation]** **At the policy level** [Formal phrase], governments must **implement** [Formal verb] **stringent regulations** [Strong collocation - strict rules] such as **carbon taxes** [Specific policy] to **penalize** [Formal verb - punish] **heavy emitters** [Specific term - those who emit a lot] and **incentivize** [Formal verb - encourage] the **transition to** [Formal phrase] **renewable energy sources** [Specific term] like **solar and wind power** [Specific examples]. **[E - Example]** **Sweden's carbon tax** [Specific real-world example], introduced in 1991, has successfully **reduced emissions by 25%** [Specific statistic] while **simultaneously growing** [Complex structure] the economy. **[R - Result]** **On an individual level** [Formal phrase], citizens can **adopt sustainable practices** [Formal phrase] such as **reducing meat consumption** [Specific action], **utilizing public transportation** [Formal phrase], and **minimizing single-use plastics** [Specific action], **collectively creating** [Complex structure] **substantial environmental benefits** [Formal phrase].

---

**[Conclusion - 50 words]**

**In conclusion** [Standard linker], climate change is **predominantly driven by** [Formal phrase - mainly caused by] **fossil fuel dependency** [Formal phrase] and requires **urgent, coordinated action** [Strong phrase]. Through **a combination of** [Formal phrase] **robust governmental policies** [Strong collocation - strong government actions] and **conscientious individual choices** [Formal phrase - thoughtful personal decisions], humanity can **mitigate the worst effects** [Formal phrase] and **transition toward** [Formal phrase] a more **sustainable future** [Common collocation].

**(275 words)**

- ✅ Ideas fully extended and well-developed

- ✅ Appropriate length (275 words)

### **✅ Coherence & Cohesion (Band 9.0):**
- ✅ Clear organization: Intro → Causes → Solutions → Conclusion

## 📚 Vocabulary Analysis

### **Problem/Cause Vocabulary:**
- **Global warming** - increase in Earth's temperature
- **Emerged as** - become recognized as
- **Pressing challenges** - urgent problems
- **Far-reaching consequences** - wide-ranging effects
- **Root causes** - fundamental causes
- **Stem primarily from** - originate mainly from
- **Anthropogenic activities** - human-caused activities
- **Chief among which** - most important of which
- **Combustion of fossil fuels** - burning of coal, oil, gas
- **Greenhouse gases** - gases that trap heat (CO2, methane)
- **Thereby trapping heat** - as a result, keeping heat in
- **Pre-industrial times** - before factories (around 1850)
- **Fossil fuel consumption** - use of coal, oil, gas
- **Relentless burning** - continuous, non-stop burning
- **Triggered** - caused, started
- **Unprecedented** - never happened before
- **Glacial melting** - ice/glaciers melting
- **Rising sea levels** - ocean levels increasing

### **Solution Vocabulary:**
- **Addressing** - dealing with, handling
- **Necessitates** - requires, makes necessary
- **Multi-faceted approach** - strategy with many parts
- **Governmental intervention** - government action/involvement
- **Individual responsibility** - personal duty/obligation
- **At the policy level** - in terms of government policy
- **Implement** - put into action, enforce
- **Stringent regulations** - strict rules
- **Carbon taxes** - taxes on carbon emissions
- **Penalize** - punish, impose penalties on
- **Heavy emitters** - those who produce lots of emissions
- **Incentivize** - encourage through rewards
- **Transition to** - change to, move to
- **Renewable energy sources** - sustainable energy (solar, wind)
- **Simultaneously growing** - at the same time as increasing
- **On an individual level** - for individual people
- **Adopt sustainable practices** - start using eco-friendly habits
- **Reducing meat consumption** - eating less meat
- **Utilizing public transportation** - using buses, trains
- **Minimizing single-use plastics** - reducing disposable plastic items
- **Collectively creating** - together producing
- **Substantial environmental benefits** - significant positive effects on environment

### **Conclusion Vocabulary:**
- **Predominantly driven by** - mainly caused by
- **Fossil fuel dependency** - reliance on coal, oil, gas
- **Urgent, coordinated action** - immediate, organized effort
- **A combination of** - a mix of
- **Robust governmental policies** - strong government strategies
- **Conscientious individual choices** - thoughtful personal decisions
- **Mitigate the worst effects** - reduce the most severe impacts
- **Transition toward** - move toward
- **Sustainable future** - eco-friendly, long-term future

## 🏗️ PEER Structure Breakdown

### **Body Paragraph 1 (Causes):**
- **P (Point):** "The root causes of climate change stem primarily from anthropogenic activities, chief among which is the combustion of fossil fuels."
- **E (Explanation):** "Coal-fired power plants and petroleum-based transportation release vast quantities of greenhouse gases..."
- **E (Example):** "According to the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, global temperatures have risen by approximately 1.1°C..."
- **R (Result):** "Consequently, this relentless burning has triggered unprecedented glacial melting and rising sea levels."

### **Body Paragraph 2 (Solutions):**
- **P (Point):** "Addressing this crisis necessitates a multi-faceted approach involving both governmental intervention and individual responsibility."
- **E (Explanation - Government):** "At the policy level, governments must implement stringent regulations such as carbon taxes..."
- **E (Example):** "Sweden's carbon tax, introduced in 1991, has successfully reduced emissions by 25%..."
- **E (Explanation - Individual):** "On an individual level, citizens can adopt sustainable practices..."
- **R (Result):** "...collectively creating substantial environmental benefits."

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

### **1. Overgeneralization:**
- ❌ "Pollution causes climate change."
- ✅ "The combustion of fossil fuels releases greenhouse gases, particularly carbon dioxide, which trap heat in the atmosphere."

### **2. Unrealistic Solutions:**
- ❌ "Governments should ban all factories."
- ✅ "Governments must implement stringent regulations such as carbon taxes to penalize heavy emitters and incentivize the transition to renewable energy."

### **3. Vague Examples:**
- ❌ "Some countries have carbon taxes."
- ✅ "Sweden's carbon tax, introduced in 1991, has successfully reduced emissions by 25% while simultaneously growing the economy."

### **4. Missing Specificity:**
- ❌ "We should use clean energy."
- ✅ "Governments should incentivize the transition to renewable energy sources like solar and wind power."

### **5. Weak Cause-Effect Links:**
- ❌ "Cars cause global warming."
- ✅ "Petroleum-based transportation releases vast quantities of greenhouse gases into the atmosphere, thereby trapping heat."

### **6. Too Short:**
- ❌ 200-240 words
- ✅ 270-290 words (ideal range)

## 💡 Key Takeaways

### **What Makes This Band 8.0-9.0:**

1. **Specific Causes:** Not just "pollution" but "combustion of fossil fuels", "greenhouse gases", "carbon dioxide"
2. **Concrete Solutions:** Carbon taxes, renewable energy transition, specific individual actions
3. **Real Examples:** IPCC data (1.1°C rise), Sweden's carbon tax (25% reduction)
4. **Multi-Level Solutions:** Government (policy) + Individual (behavior change)
5. **Cause-Effect Links:** "thereby trapping heat", "Consequently", "has triggered"
6. **Academic Tone:** "anthropogenic", "necessitates", "multi-faceted approach"
7. **PEER Structure:** Every paragraph has Point, Explanation, Example, Result
8. **Appropriate Length:** 275 words (well above 250 minimum)

### **Problem-Solution Essay Template:**

**Introduction:**
- "[Problem] has emerged as one of the most pressing challenges..."
- "This essay will explore the primary drivers of this phenomenon and propose viable solutions..."

**Body 1 (Causes):**
- "The root causes of [problem] stem primarily from..."
- "According to [source], [specific data]..."
- "Consequently, this has triggered [specific consequences]..."

**Body 2 (Solutions):**
- "Addressing this crisis necessitates a multi-faceted approach involving both [X] and [Y]..."
- "At the policy level, governments must implement [specific policy]..."
- "[Country's] [policy], introduced in [year], has successfully [result]..."
- "On an individual level, citizens can adopt [specific actions]..."

**Conclusion:**
- "In conclusion, [problem] is predominantly driven by [cause] and requires urgent action..."
- "Through a combination of robust governmental policies and conscientious individual choices, we can mitigate the worst effects..."
