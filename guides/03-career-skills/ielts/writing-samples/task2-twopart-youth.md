---

## 📝 The Question

**In many countries, young people are spending more time on leisure activities and less time on developing professional skills. Why is this happening? What can be done to encourage them to focus more on career development?**

---

## 🎯 Essay Structure Analysis

This is a **Two-Part Question** requiring:
1. **Body 1:** Answer Question 1 - Why are young people prioritizing leisure over skill development? (Causes/Reasons)
2. **Body 2:** Answer Question 2 - What solutions can encourage career focus? (Solutions/Recommendations)

**Strategy:** 
- Question 1 focuses on CAUSES (similar to Problem-Solution essays)
- Question 2 focuses on SOLUTIONS
- Don't give your opinion on whether this is good/bad—just analyze WHY and propose HOW to change

---

## ✅ Band 8.0+ Model Answer

### **Introduction**

The contemporary trend of young people dedicating disproportionate time to entertainment rather than acquiring marketable skills has become increasingly prevalent across developed nations. **[Context - paraphrases question]** This essay will examine the underlying factors driving this shift and propose practical measures to redirect youth attention toward professional development. **[Thesis - announces both parts]**

### **Body Paragraph 1: Reasons for This Trend**

Several interconnected factors explain why young people prioritize leisure over career preparation. **[Topic sentence - answers Q1]** Primarily, the proliferation of digital entertainment platforms—from streaming services to gaming ecosystems—has created an environment of constant, easily accessible distraction. **[Reason 1]** Unlike skill development, which requires sustained effort and delayed gratification, these platforms offer immediate pleasure with minimal cognitive investment, making them inherently more appealing to young minds. **[Explanation - contrasts leisure vs. skill building]** Furthermore, mounting economic uncertainty has paradoxically diminished motivation for career investment. **[Reason 2]** Many young people observe previous generations struggling despite educational credentials, leading them to question whether intensive career preparation guarantees financial security. **[Explanation with generational context]** Consequently, some adopt a "live in the moment" philosophy, prioritizing present enjoyment over uncertain future gains. **[Result]** Additionally, societal pressure has shifted—whereas previous eras emphasized professional achievement, modern culture increasingly validates lifestyle choices centered on personal fulfillment and work-life balance, inadvertently downplaying traditional career ambition. **[Reason 3 - cultural shift]**

### **Body Paragraph 2: Solutions to Encourage Career Focus**

To counteract this trend, a multifaceted approach involving educational institutions, employers, and policymakers is essential. **[Topic sentence - answers Q2]** Educational systems should integrate practical, industry-relevant training earlier in curricula, demonstrating clear pathways between skill acquisition and tangible career outcomes. **[Solution 1]** For example, vocational programs combining classroom learning with paid apprenticeships have proven effective in countries like Germany and Switzerland, where youth unemployment remains exceptionally low. **[Example showing solution effectiveness]** Moreover, employers could offer structured mentorship programs and transparent career progression frameworks, helping young people visualize long-term benefits of skill investment. **[Solution 2]** Finally, governments might provide financial incentives—such as tax credits or subsidized certification programs—for young people pursuing professional qualifications, thereby reducing economic barriers to career development. **[Solution 3]** These interventions collectively address both the attraction of leisure and the perceived futility of career preparation. **[Links back to causes]**

### **Conclusion**

In summary, the shift toward leisure over professional development stems from digital distractions, economic uncertainty, and evolving cultural values. **[Restate causes]** However, through integrated educational reforms, employer engagement, and governmental support, societies can effectively guide youth toward balanced lives that incorporate both enjoyment and purposeful career building. **[Restate solutions + forward-looking statement]**

## 🎓 Grammar Structures Used (Band 8.0+)

### **1. Present Perfect (for trends continuing to present):**
- "...has become increasingly prevalent across developed nations."
- "...has created an environment of constant distraction."
- "...societal pressure has shifted..."

### **2. Passive Voice (for academic tone):**
- "...where skill acquisition **is integrated** early..."
- "...have **proven** effective in countries..."

### **3. Complex Sentences with Multiple Clauses:**
- "Unlike skill development, which requires sustained effort and delayed gratification, these platforms offer immediate pleasure with minimal cognitive investment, making them inherently more appealing to young minds."
  - (Main clause + relative clause + participle clause)

### **4. Participle Clauses (conciseness):**
- "...**demonstrating** clear pathways between skill acquisition and tangible career outcomes."
- "...**helping** young people visualize long-term benefits..."
- "...**leading** them to question whether intensive career preparation guarantees security."

### **5. Modal Verbs (for recommendations):**
- "Educational systems **should** integrate practical training..."
- "Employers **could** offer structured mentorship..."
- "Governments **might** provide financial incentives..."

### **6. Relative Clauses (adding information):**
- "...whereas previous eras emphasized professional achievement..."
- "...countries like Germany and Switzerland, **where** youth unemployment remains exceptionally low."

### **7. Gerunds & Infinitives:**
- "**Prioritizing** present enjoyment over uncertain future gains..." (gerund)
- "**To counteract** this trend..." (infinitive of purpose)
- "...effective in **guiding** youth toward..." (gerund after preposition)

### **8. Nominalization (academic style):**
- "...the **proliferation** of digital entertainment..." (verb "proliferate" → noun)
- "...question whether intensive career **preparation** guarantees..." (verb "prepare" → noun)
- "...clear **pathways** between skill **acquisition** and..." (verbs → nouns)

### **9. Contrast Structures:**
- "Unlike X, Y..." (Unlike skill development, platforms offer...)
- "Whereas X, Y..." (Whereas previous eras emphasized achievement, modern culture validates lifestyle)

## ✏️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

### **Structure Mistakes (Two-Part Essays):**

| ❌ Wrong Approach | ✅ Correct Approach | Why? |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Mixing causes and solutions in one paragraph | Separate paragraph for Q1 (causes) and Q2 (solutions) | Clearer structure, easier to follow |
| Giving opinion when not asked | Just answer WHY and WHAT, not "is this good/bad?" | Follow question instructions exactly |
| Solutions not addressing causes | Link solutions to specific causes mentioned | Shows logical thinking |
| Forgetting to answer one question | Answer BOTH why + what can be done | Task Response drops to 5-6 if incomplete |

### **Grammar Mistakes:**

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why? |
|:---|:---|:---|
| "Young people is spending time" | "Young people are spending time" | Plural subject needs plural verb |
| "They prefer to playing games" | "They prefer to play games / prefer playing games" | "Prefer to + infinitive" or "prefer + gerund" |
| "Because of young people lazy" | "Because young people are unmotivated" | Complete sentence needed after "because" |
| "Government should to help" | "The government should help" | Modal + base verb (no "to"), add article |
| "This makes them not want to work" | "This makes them unwilling to work / discourages them from working" | More natural phrasing |

### **Vocabulary Mistakes:**

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why? |
|:---|:---|:---|
| "Young people are lazy" | "Young people lack motivation / are demotivated" | Avoid judgmental language, more analytical |
| "They don't want to work hard" | "They prioritize leisure / are less career-focused" | More academic, neutral tone |
| "Fun activities" | "Leisure activities / entertainment" | More formal vocabulary |
| "Learn job skills" | "Acquire professional skills / develop marketable competencies" | More sophisticated |
| "Make young people study" | "Encourage / motivate / incentivize young people to..." | Less forceful, more nuanced |

### **Content Mistakes:**

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why? |
|:---|:---|:---|
| "Young people are just lazy and don't care" | Analyze deeper causes (technology, economy, culture) | Shows critical thinking, avoids stereotypes |
| "Schools should force students to study" | "Educational systems should integrate..., demonstrate..." | More realistic, specific solutions |
| No examples | Germany/Switzerland apprenticeships, specific outcomes | Evidence strengthens argument |
| One-sentence explanation | Full development: cause → explanation → example/result | Meets TR requirements |

## 💭 Brainstorming: SHEEP Method Applied

### **Question 1: Why prioritize leisure over skills? (Causes)**

Using **SHEEP** (Social, Health, Economic, Environment, Personal):

- **S (Social):** Cultural shift toward work-life balance, peer pressure to enjoy youth, social media influence
- **H (Health):** Stress from academic pressure leads to escapism, mental health awareness emphasizes balance
- **E (Economic):** Uncertain job market ("why bother?"), gig economy reduces need for formal credentials, high cost of education
- **E (Environment):** N/A (not relevant to this topic)
- **P (Personal):** Instant gratification from tech, fear of failure/commitment, lack of role models, unclear career paths

**Choose 2-3 strongest points** that you can explain well.

### **Question 2: What can encourage career focus? (Solutions)**

- **Education:** Earlier vocational training, practical curricula, career counseling, show clear skill-to-job pathways
- **Employers:** Mentorship programs, internships, transparent career ladders, competitive pay for entry-level
- **Government:** Subsidize training, tax incentives for skill development, public awareness campaigns
- **Parents/Society:** Role modeling, celebrating achievement, reducing stigma around vocational careers
- **Technology:** Gamify learning, online skill platforms, AI career matching

**Match solutions to causes** for better coherence!

## ⏱️ Time Management (40 minutes)

- **5 mins:** Analyze question carefully - What are the TWO parts? (Why + What solutions)
- **5 mins:** Brainstorm - List 3 causes, 3 solutions, think of examples
- **25 mins:** Write
  - Introduction (3 mins) - paraphrase question, state structure
  - Body 1 - Causes (10 mins) - 3 reasons with explanations
  - Body 2 - Solutions (10 mins) - 3 solutions with examples
  - Conclusion (2 mins) - summarize causes + solutions
- **5 mins:** Proofread - Check both questions answered, grammar, spelling, word count

**Pro tip:** For "Why?" questions, always explain BEYOND the obvious. Don't just say "technology" - say WHY technology causes this specific behavior!

## 📖 Examiner's Perspective

**What examiners LOVE to see:**
- ✅ Deep analysis of causes (not superficial "they're lazy")
- ✅ Specific, actionable solutions (not vague "government should do something")
- ✅ Real-world examples (Germany/Switzerland, specific programs)
- ✅ Logical links between causes and solutions
- ✅ Academic vocabulary used naturally
- ✅ Balanced, objective tone (not preachy or judgmental)

**What causes score deductions:**
- ❌ Only answering one question (Why OR What, not both)
- ❌ Stereotyping or judgmental language ("young people are lazy/stupid")
- ❌ Vague, generic solutions with no detail
- ❌ No examples or evidence
- ❌ Poor paraphrasing (copying question words)
- ❌ Giving opinion when not asked

**Hidden bonus:** Examiners love when you show critical thinking by analyzing WHY something happens psychologically or economically, not just stating the obvious!
