## 1️⃣ Supply & Demand - The Foundation

### **The Most Important Graph in Economics:**

```
Price
  ↑
  │     Supply (S)
  │        ╱
  │      ╱
  │    ╱
P*├───✖───── Equilibrium (E)
  │  ╱  ╲
  │╱      ╲
  │         ╲ Demand (D)
  └─────────────→ Quantity
           Q*
```

**Equilibrium:** Where Supply = Demand → Market clears at Price P*, Quantity Q*

---

### **Law of Demand:**

**"When price ↑, quantity demanded ↓" (ceteris paribus - all else equal)**

**Why?**
1. **Substitution Effect:** Khi iPhone đắt hơn → Chuyển sang Android
2. **Income Effect:** Giá tăng → Thu nhập thực giảm → Mua ít hơn

**Demand Curve Shifts (D → D'):**

```
Demand INCREASES (shift right) when:
├── Income ↑ (for normal goods)
├── Price of substitutes ↑
├── Price of complements ↓
├── Consumer preferences change (trends)
├── Population ↑
└── Expectations of future price ↑

Example: COVID → Work from home
→ Demand for webcams, monitors SHIFTS RIGHT
→ Price & quantity both ↑
```

---

### **Law of Supply:**

**"When price ↑, quantity supplied ↑"**

**Why?** Higher price = Higher profit incentive for producers

**Supply Curve Shifts (S → S'):**

```
Supply INCREASES (shift right) when:
├── Technology improves (lower costs)
├── Input prices ↓ (oil, labor, materials)
├── Number of sellers ↑
├── Subsidies from government
└── Expectations of future price ↓

Example: GPU shortage (2021-2022)
→ Crypto mining + Gaming demand ↑
→ Supply couldn't keep up (semiconductor shortage)
→ Prices 2-3x MSRP
→ 2023: Crypto crash → Demand ↓ → Prices normalize
```

---

### **Market Equilibrium:**

```
Surplus (Excess Supply):
├── Price > Equilibrium → Producers make too much
├── Inventory piles up
└── → Prices forced down (clearance sales)

Shortage (Excess Demand):
├── Price < Equilibrium → Consumers want more than available
├── Empty shelves
└── → Prices forced up (scalping, black market)

Example: PS5 Launch (2020)
├── Sony priced at $499 (below equilibrium)
├── Massive shortage
├── Scalpers resold at $800-1000
└── Market equilibrium actually ~$700-800
```

---

### **Real-World Applications:**

#### **Tech Layoffs (2022-2023):**
```
Supply side:
├── Tech companies hired aggressively (2020-2021)
├── Oversupply of roles
└── → Equilibrium salary high

Demand side:
├── Fed raised rates → Tech valuations ↓
├── Companies cut costs → Layoffs
└── → Demand for tech workers ↓

Result:
├── Thousands laid off = Supply ↑
├── Fewer open roles = Demand ↓
└── → Salaries & leverage down (new equilibrium)
```

#### **Nvidia GPUs (AI Boom 2023-2024):**
```
Demand side:
├── ChatGPT launch → AI gold rush
├── Every company wants AI
└── → Demand for H100 GPUs explodes

Supply side:
├── Nvidia can't scale fast enough (TSMC capacity)
├── 6-12 month lead times
└── → Supply constrained

Result:
├── Prices skyrocket
├── Nvidia stock 10x
└── → Competitors rush in (AMD, Intel, startups)
```

## 2️⃣ Elasticity - Price Sensitivity

### **Price Elasticity of Demand (PED):**

**Formula:**
```
PED = % Change in Quantity Demanded / % Change in Price

Example:
Price ↑ 10% → Quantity ↓ 20%
PED = -20% / 10% = -2 (elastic)
```

---

### **Classification:**

| PED Value | Type | Meaning | Examples |
|-----------|------|---------|----------|
| **PED > 1** | Elastic | Sensitive to price | Luxury goods, restaurants, vacations |
| **PED = 1** | Unit elastic | Proportional | Some clothing |
| **PED < 1** | Inelastic | NOT sensitive | Insulin, gas (short-term), utilities |
| **PED = 0** | Perfectly inelastic | Buy same amount regardless | Life-saving medicine |
| **PED = ∞** | Perfectly elastic | Won't buy if price ↑ at all | Identical commodities |

---

### **Factors Affecting Elasticity:**

```
More ELASTIC (PED high) when:
├── Many substitutes available
├── Luxury / Non-essential
├── Large % of budget
└── Long time horizon (can adjust)

More INELASTIC (PED low) when:
├── Few/no substitutes
├── Necessity
├── Small % of budget
└── Short time horizon
```

---

### **Applications:**

#### **Pricing Power = Inelastic Demand = Moat**

```
Company with INELASTIC demand can raise prices freely:

Apple iPhone:
├── Brand loyalty strong
├── Ecosystem lock-in (iMessage, iCloud)
├── Few true substitutes (for Apple users)
└── → PED low → Pricing power → High margins

VS

Generic Android phone:
├── Many identical competitors
├── Easy to switch
├── Price-sensitive customers
└── → PED high → Price competition → Low margins
```

#### **SaaS Pricing Strategy:**

```
Inelastic segments (Enterprise):
├── Mission-critical software
├── High switching costs
├── Training, integrations
└── → Charge premium ($10k-100k/year)

Elastic segments (SMB):
├── Many alternatives
├── Easy to switch
├── Price-sensitive
└── → Freemium or low-cost ($29-99/month)
```

---

### **Income Elasticity:**

```
Income Elasticity = % Change in Quantity / % Change in Income

Normal Goods (YED > 0):
├── Income ↑ → Demand ↑
└── Examples: Dining out, travel, tech gadgets

Inferior Goods (YED < 0):
├── Income ↑ → Demand ↓
└── Examples: Instant noodles, used cars, budget airlines

Luxury Goods (YED > 1):
├── Income ↑ 10% → Demand ↑ 20%+
└── Examples: Rolex, Tesla, first-class flights
```

**Investment Implication:**
```
During economic expansion:
→ Invest in luxury/discretionary (high YED)
→ Tesla, LVMH, travel stocks

During recession:
→ Invest in necessities/inferior goods (low/negative YED)
→ Walmart, Dollar General, generic brands
```

## 3️⃣ Market Structures

### **Spectrum of Competition:**

```
Perfect Competition → Monopolistic → Oligopoly → Monopoly
(Many firms)                                    (One firm)
(No pricing power)                         (Full pricing power)
```

---

### **1. Perfect Competition (Rare in real world)**

**Characteristics:**
- Many buyers & sellers
- Identical products (homogeneous)
- Free entry/exit
- Perfect information
- Price takers (no pricing power)

**Examples:**
- Agricultural commodities (wheat, corn)
- Stock market (for liquid stocks)
- Forex market

**Profit:**
- Short-term: Can make profit
- Long-term: Zero economic profit (new entrants compete away)

**Investment takeaway:**
- ❌ Avoid investing in perfect competition
- No moat → No pricing power → Commoditized

---

### **2. Monopolistic Competition (Most common)**

**Characteristics:**
- Many firms
- Differentiated products (branding, features)
- Some pricing power
- Low barriers to entry

**Examples:**
- Restaurants (each unique, but many options)
- Clothing brands
- Mobile apps (millions on App Store)
- Most tech startups

**Profit:**
- Short-term: Can make profit via differentiation
- Long-term: Competed down (but not to zero due to differentiation)

**Investment takeaway:**
- ⚠️ Depends on strength of differentiation
- Strong brand = Moat (Nike, Lululemon)
- Weak brand = Commoditized (generic clothing)

---

### **3. Oligopoly (Few dominant players)**

**Characteristics:**
- Few large firms (typically 3-8)
- High barriers to entry (capital, tech, network effects)
- Interdependent (each firm's actions affect others)
- Pricing power (but watch for collusion)

**Examples:**
- **Tech:** Cloud (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud)
- **Telecom:** Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile
- **Airlines:** United, Delta, American
- **Soda:** Coca-Cola, Pepsi
- **Smartphones:** Apple, Samsung, (Google)

**Game Theory:**
- Firms must consider rivals' reactions
- Can lead to price wars OR tacit collusion

**Investment takeaway:**
- ✅ Good for investors if:
  - High barriers to entry
  - Rational competitors (don't price war)
  - Growing market
- ❌ Bad if: Commoditized, price wars, low switching costs

---

### **4. Monopoly (One dominant firm)**

**Characteristics:**
- Single seller
- No close substitutes
- High barriers to entry
- Price maker (sets prices)

**Examples:**
- **Natural Monopoly:** Utilities (electric, water) - high fixed costs
- **Tech Monopoly:** 
  - Google Search (90% market share)
  - Windows OS (desktop)
  - Facebook (social network effect)
- **Patent Monopoly:** Pharmaceuticals (20-year exclusivity)

**Profit:**
- Can earn supernormal profits indefinitely (if moat holds)

**Investment takeaway:**
- ✅ **Best for investors** (if legal)
- Maximum pricing power
- Sustainable competitive advantage
- High ROI, margins

**Risks:**
- Government regulation/antitrust
- Technological disruption
- Public backlash

---

### **Market Power Spectrum Analysis:**

```
Analyze companies using this framework:

Step 1: Identify market structure
├── How many competitors?
├── Product differentiation?
└── Barriers to entry?

Step 2: Assess pricing power
├── Can they raise prices 10% without losing customers?
├── Check gross margin trends (↑ = pricing power)
└── Compare to competitors

Step 3: Identify moat source
├── Network effects (Facebook)
├── Switching costs (SAP, Oracle)
├── Brand (Apple, Nike)
├── Economies of scale (Walmart, Amazon)
└── Patents/IP (Pharma)

Investment decision:
├── Monopoly/Strong Oligopoly + Growing market = BUY
├── Monopolistic Competition + Weak brand = AVOID
└── Perfect Competition = NEVER BUY
```

## 4️⃣ Consumer Behavior & Utility

### **Utility Theory:**

**Utility** = Satisfaction/happiness derived from consuming a good

**Total Utility (TU):** Sum of satisfaction from all units consumed

**Marginal Utility (MU):** Additional satisfaction from consuming one more unit

```
Example: Eating pizza slices

Slice | Total Utility | Marginal Utility
------|---------------|------------------
  1   |      10       |      10
  2   |      18       |       8
  3   |      24       |       6
  4   |      28       |       4
  5   |      30       |       2
  6   |      30       |       0
  7   |      28       |      -2 (sick)
```

---

### **Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility:**

**"Each additional unit consumed provides LESS additional satisfaction"**

**Why it matters:**
```
Pricing implication:
├── First iPhone: High utility → Willing to pay $1000
├── Second iPhone: Lower MU → Only pay $500
├── Third iPhone: Even lower MU → Pay $100
└── → Demand curve slopes downward
```

**Applications:**

#### **Subscription Pricing:**
```
Netflix unlimited streaming:
├── User pays $15/month
├── Can watch infinite content
├── BUT: Diminishing MU after 10 hours/week
└── → Most users don't "over-consume" despite unlimited
    → Profitable for Netflix
```

#### **Buffet Restaurants:**
```
All-you-can-eat buffet:
├── First plate: High MU
├── Second plate: Lower MU
├── Third plate: Much lower MU
└── → Most people stop before eating their "money's worth"
    → Profitable for restaurant
```

---

### **Budget Constraint:**

```
Consumer has limited income (I)
Must allocate between goods (X, Y)

Budget Line:
I = Px * X + Py * Y

Example:
Income = $1000/month
Px (dining out) = $50/meal
Py (groceries) = $100/week

Trade-off:
├── All dining: 20 meals/month
├── All groceries: 10 weeks (2.3 months)
└── Mix: E.g., 10 meals + 5 weeks groceries
```

**Optimal Consumption:**
- Consumer maximizes utility given budget constraint
- Choose where MU per dollar spent is equal across all goods

---

### **Tech Worker Behavior:**

```
High-income tech workers:
├── Budget constraint relaxed
├── Time becomes the scarce resource
└── → Willing to pay premium for convenience

Examples:
├── Meal delivery (DoorDash) > Cooking
├── Uber > Public transit
├── Premium tools (Superhuman email, Notion Pro)
└── Outsourcing (cleaning, laundry)

Investment implication:
→ Convenience economy grows with high-earner population
```

## 5️⃣ Production & Costs

### **Production Function:**

```
Output (Q) = f(Labor, Capital, Technology)

Short-run: Capital fixed, vary labor
Long-run: All inputs variable
```

---

### **Types of Costs:**

| Cost Type | Definition | Example (Software Company) |
|-----------|------------|----------------------------|
| **Fixed Costs (FC)** | Don't change with output | Office rent, salaries, servers |
| **Variable Costs (VC)** | Change with output | AWS costs (per user), support staff |
| **Total Cost (TC)** | FC + VC | All costs combined |
| **Average Total Cost (ATC)** | TC / Q | Cost per user |
| **Marginal Cost (MC)** | Change in TC from one more unit | Cost to serve one more user |

---

### **Cost Curves:**

```
Cost
  ↑
  │        MC
  │       ╱│╲
  │     ╱  │  ╲ ATC
  │   ╱    │    ╲
  │ ╱      │      ╲
  │╱       │        ╲
  └────────┴──────────→ Quantity
        Q_min ATC
```

**Key insights:**
1. **MC cuts ATC at minimum point**
2. **Economies of Scale:** ATC decreasing (increasing returns)
3. **Diseconomies of Scale:** ATC increasing (decreasing returns)

---

### **Economies of Scale:**

**Definition:** Average cost ↓ as output ↑

**Sources:**
```
Technical economies:
├── Fixed costs spread over more units
├── Specialization of labor
└── Bulk purchasing power

Example: Netflix
├── Fixed cost: Content library ($17B/year)
├── Variable cost: Bandwidth (~$2/user/year)
├── 250M subscribers
└── → ATC = $17B / 250M + $2 = $70/user/year
    → Add 50M users → ATC drops to $55/user
```

**Why tech companies scale so well:**
```
Software has near-zero marginal cost:
├── Fixed: Development, servers
├── Variable: Almost nothing (bandwidth, support)
└── → Massive economies of scale → Winner-takes-most
```

---

### **SaaS Unit Economics:**

```
Key metrics:

CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost):
├── Marketing spend / New customers
├── Example: $300/customer

LTV (Lifetime Value):
├── (Monthly Revenue per Customer) x (Gross Margin %) x (Lifetime months)
├── Example: $50/mo x 80% margin x 36 months = $1,440

LTV/CAC Ratio:
├── $1,440 / $300 = 4.8x
├── Good: >3x
├── Great: >5x
└── Bad: <1x (losing money on each customer)

Payback Period:
├── CAC / (Monthly Revenue x Gross Margin)
├── $300 / ($50 x 80%) = 7.5 months
├── Good: <12 months
└── Great: <6 months
```

**Investment Analysis:**
```
When analyzing SaaS companies, check:
├── ✅ LTV/CAC > 3x
├── ✅ Gross margin > 70%
├── ✅ Payback < 12 months
├── ✅ Negative churn (expansions > cancellations)
└── ✅ Economies of scale kicking in (ATC decreasing)
```

## 6️⃣ Market Failures

**When free markets DON'T lead to optimal outcomes:**

### **1. Externalities**

**Negative Externality:** Cost imposed on third parties not involved in transaction

```
Example: Factory pollution
├── Factory produces goods, sells to consumers
├── BUT: Pollutes air (costs borne by neighbors)
├── Social cost > Private cost
└── → Market over-produces (ignores external cost)

Tech example: Social media
├── Facebook provides free service
├── BUT: Mental health issues, misinformation, polarization
├── Society bears costs (not reflected in FB pricing)
└── → Over-consumption (too much social media use)
```

**Positive Externality:** Benefit to third parties

```
Example: Education
├── Individual pays for college
├── BUT: Society benefits (informed citizens, innovation)
├── Social benefit > Private benefit
└── → Market under-produces (individual doesn't capture full value)

Tech example: Open source software
├── Developer creates free tool
├── Millions benefit
└── → Under-produced (developer not fully compensated)
```

**Solutions:**
- Taxes (negative externalities) → Carbon tax
- Subsidies (positive externalities) → Education subsidies, R&D credits
- Regulation → Emission standards

---

### **2. Public Goods**

**Characteristics:**
- **Non-excludable:** Can't prevent people from using
- **Non-rivalrous:** One person's use doesn't reduce availability

**Examples:**
- National defense
- Clean air
- Public parks
- Open source software
- Wikipedia

**Problem: Free Rider Problem**
```
Rational individual:
├── Benefit from public good
├── BUT: Don't pay (others will)
└── → If everyone thinks this → Under-provision

Example: Open source
├── Millions use Linux, React, Python
├── Few contribute code/money
└── → Relies on volunteer labor or corporate sponsorship
```

---

### **3. Information Asymmetry**

**One party has more information than the other**

#### **Adverse Selection (Before transaction):**

```
Example: Used car market ("Lemons problem")
├── Seller knows car quality
├── Buyer doesn't
├── → Buyer assumes average quality
├── → Good car owners won't sell (price too low)
└── → Only bad cars ("lemons") sold → Market collapses

Tech example: Hiring
├── Candidate knows their true skills
├── Employer must infer from resume/interview
├── → Adverse selection risk
└── → Solutions: Coding tests, referrals, trial periods
```

#### **Moral Hazard (After transaction):**

```
Example: Insurance
├── Person buys health insurance
├── → Takes more health risks (insured anyway)
└── → Insurance company loses money

Tech example: Equity compensation
├── Employees get stock options
├── → May take excessive risks (upside to them, downside to company)
└── → Solution: Vesting schedules, clawbacks
```

**Solutions:**
- Signaling (education, certifications)
- Screening (background checks, trials)
- Warranties/guarantees
- Reputation systems (reviews, ratings)

## 7️⃣ Applications for Investors & Tech Workers

### **For Stock Investors:**

#### **Competitive Moat Checklist (Microeconomics View):**

```
Analyze companies through micro lens:

1. Market Structure:
   ├── [ ] Monopoly/strong oligopoly?
   ├── [ ] High barriers to entry?
   └── [ ] Rational competitors?

2. Pricing Power (Elasticity):
   ├── [ ] Can raise prices 10% without losing share?
   ├── [ ] Check gross margin trends (↑ = power)
   └── [ ] PED < 1 (inelastic demand)?

3. Economies of Scale:
   ├── [ ] Fixed costs high, variable low?
   ├── [ ] ATC decreasing as scale ↑?
   └── [ ] Network effects?

4. Switching Costs:
   ├── [ ] Expensive/hard for customers to leave?
   ├── [ ] Lock-in via integration, data, training?
   └── [ ] Check retention rate >90%?

5. Market Failures Exploited:
   ├── [ ] Solving information asymmetry?
   ├── [ ] Creating positive externalities?
   └── [ ] Network effects = public good benefits?

Score > 4/5 = Strong moat → Consider investing
```

### **For Tech Workers:**

#### **Your Career as a Market:**

```
YOU are the supplier:
├── Skills = Product
├── Experience = Differentiation
├── Reputation = Brand
└── Network = Distribution

COMPANIES are buyers:
├── Demand for your skillset
├── Willingness to pay (salary)
└── Substitutes (other candidates)

Market dynamics:
├── Elastic demand (commodity skills) = Low pay
├── Inelastic demand (rare skills) = High pay
└── Oligopsony (few buyers) = Lower salaries
```

**Strategies:**

```
1. Increase demand for YOUR supply:
   ├── Learn scarce skills (AI, Rust, distributed systems)
   ├── Build public portfolio (GitHub, blog)
   └── Network effects (referrals, reputation)

2. Reduce elasticity (make yourself unique):
   ├── Niche specialization (not "generic dev")
   ├── Domain expertise (fintech, healthcare, gaming)
   └── Combination of skills (ML + Backend + Product sense)

3. Increase your bargaining power:
   ├── Multiple offers = competitive bidding
   ├── Remote work = geographic arbitrage
   └── Build "monopoly" on rare skill combo

4. Exploit market failures:
   ├── Information asymmetry: Better negotiation tactics
   ├── Employer doesn't know your true value → Signal via:
       ├── Projects, open source, talks
       └── Certifications, degrees
```

---

### **For Entrepreneurs:**

#### **Pricing Strategy Framework:**

```
Step 1: Assess market structure
├── Perfect competition → Cost-based pricing (no choice)
├── Monopolistic → Value-based pricing (differentiation)
└── Monopoly → Value-based + Price discrimination

Step 2: Measure price elasticity
├── Test with A/B pricing
├── Survey willingness to pay
└── PED high → Price low, volume high
    PED low → Price high, volume lower

Step 3: Segment customers
├── Enterprise (inelastic) → Charge premium
├── SMB (elastic) → Freemium/low-cost
└── Consumers (very elastic) → Ad-supported/free

Step 4: Price discrimination
├── Student discounts (lower willingness to pay)
├── Geographic pricing (India vs US)
├── Volume discounts (economies of scale pass-through)
└── Time-based (early bird, surge pricing)
```

---

#### **Build Economic Moats:**

```
Microeconomics moat playbook:

1. Create switching costs:
   ├── Integrations (Salesforce, Slack)
   ├── Data lock-in (accumulated history)
   └── Team training/workflows

2. Network effects:
   ├── Marketplaces (Uber, Airbnb)
   ├── Communication (WhatsApp, Discord)
   └── Platforms (iOS, Shopify)

3. Economies of scale:
   ├── High fixed costs (R&D, content)
   ├── Low marginal costs (software)
   └── → Small players can't compete

4. Exploit information asymmetry:
   ├── Be the trusted source (reviews, ratings)
   ├── Reduce buyer uncertainty
   └── → Carfax, Yelp, TripAdvisor model

5. Solve externalities:
   ├── Positive: Create platform for value creation
   ├── Negative: Be the solution (carbon offsets, privacy)
   └── → Societal need → Pricing power
```

## 💡 KEY TAKEAWAYS

```
1. Supply & Demand drives everything
   └── Price signals, market clearing, shortages/surpluses

2. Elasticity = Pricing power
   └── Inelastic demand = Moat = High margins

3. Market structure determines profitability
   └── Monopoly > Oligopoly > Monopolistic > Perfect competition

4. Economies of scale = Tech's superpower
   └── Near-zero marginal cost → Winner-takes-most

5. Market failures = Investment opportunities
   └── Solve externalities, asymmetries, public goods

6. You are a product in a market
   └── Differentiate, reduce elasticity, build moat

7. Microeconomics + Macroeconomics = Complete picture
   └── Micro = Company analysis | Macro = Market timing
```
